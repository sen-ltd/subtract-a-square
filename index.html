Subtract-a-square take 1, 4, 9, 16, …

A heap of tokens. On your turn, take away a square number of tokens from one heap. Whoever takes the last token wins. Golomb called it "remarkably complex" in 1966 and nobody has a formula yet. The losing heaps (cold positions) 0, 2, 5, 7, 10, 12, 15, 17, 20, 22, 34, … never differ by a square, so they are a square-difference-free set — the kind of set the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem says has density 0. Yet they are about as dense as the best constructions number theorists know: roughly n0.7 of the first n numbers.
